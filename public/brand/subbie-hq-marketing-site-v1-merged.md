# Subbie HQ — Marketing Website Content, v1 (Merged Draft)

## Positioning note

Merges the outcome-first brief with the earlier competitive-positioning
draft. Kept from the brief: the "Before You Sign / While You're On Site /
When It's Time To Claim / When A Dispute Happens" journey structure,
benefit-led headlines, tone ("an experienced Contracts Manager explaining
things over coffee"). Kept from the earlier draft: the differentiation
against main-contractor-first competitors (Payapps, Buildlogic, GCPay)
and sole-trader-friendly pricing.

**On removing AI references:** mostly agreed — nothing here leads with
"AI-powered." But one honest, plain mention is kept (see the "How It
Works" section on the homepage and the note on the Contract Review page),
because the app's own Contract Review carries a "not legal advice"
disclaimer specifically because it's automated analysis, not a human
lawyer's opinion. If the marketing never says that, a subcontractor could
reasonably expect a fully human-reviewed legal opinion and be genuinely
caught off guard once they're actually using the product — a worse trust
problem than the word "AI" itself. The mention is placed low-key, once,
in service of setting that expectation honestly — not as a selling point.

**Scope note:** this is v1 — a real, complete site, not a stub. The full
brief describes 9 feature pages, 8 industry pages, 12+ problem pages, 4
comparison pages, and a 100+ article resource centre. That's an ongoing
content operation, not a single build. V1 covers the homepage, pricing,
the 3 feature pages tied to what's actually built and working today, 2
industry pages establishing the template for the rest, 1 comparison page,
and the 2 SEO/problem pieces already drafted. Everything else is listed
as a Phase 2 roadmap at the end of this document — deliberately not
batch-generated, since several of those topics (Liquidated Damages,
Extension of Time, Construction Contract Administration) touch real
legislation and deserve individual care, not a generation pass.

**The Problem**
Most subcontract "reviews" are a lawyer's invoice you can't afford, or a
gut feeling you hope is right. Somewhere in a 60-page document is a
clause about notice periods, retention, or set-off rights that could cost
you real money if you miss it.

**The Consequence**
Missed notice deadlines mean lost entitlement. Unfamiliar payment
preconditions mean delayed cash flow. And nobody finds out until it's
already too late to do anything about it.

**How Subbie HQ Helps**
Upload your subcontract. Get a plain-English breakdown of what's
genuinely different from standard terms — ranked by what actually
matters commercially, not just how many clauses changed. Every finding

renegotiate.

You'll also see what's working in your favour — an honest picture, not
a wall of red flags.

**Expected Outcome**
You start the job knowing exactly what's expected of you, and exactly
what to keep an eye on — the same starting point an experienced
Contracts Manager would give you.

*This is an automated analysis of possible contract differences. It is

**Problem/SEO pages — organised into four groups** (per further product

⚠️ **Jurisdictional caveat — read before writing any of these:** several
topics below (Adjudication, Time Bars, Retentions, Payment Claims) don't
just vary by topic, they vary by JURISDICTION. NZ has one Construction
Contracts Act. Australia has separate Security of Payment legislation per
state, and the specific rules differ from each other and from NZ's
regime. A single generic article risks being flatly wrong for roughly
half the audience depending which side of the Tasman they're reading
from. Each flagged topic below needs either two versions (NZ + AU) or a
single version explicitly scoped to one jurisdiction with a clear note
that the other differs — never written as if the rules are universal.
This applies on top of the existing "verify against real legislation
before publishing" rule already in place for legal content.
